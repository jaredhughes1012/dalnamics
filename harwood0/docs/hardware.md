# Hardware

harwood0 is an indoor mecanum rover with a work table on top and a four-axis arm (yaw, shoulder, elbow, and a parallel gripper) on a tower that rides a track along the right-hand side. One camera looks forward out of the nose; a second rides on the gripper. It is built for easy assembly rather than strength. Parts snap or screw together, with self-tapping screws and no glue, heat-set inserts, or bearings.

![Assembly](../cad/renders/assembly.png)

Renders are generated by `make render` (see [CAD workflow](#cad-workflow)).

## Layout

- **Chassis.** A flat box with a rounded nose, printed in one piece.
  - Four GA25 gearmotors bolt through two lengthwise inner walls by their gearbox faces, each driving a mecanum wheel in an arched well.
  - The battery, Uno, and both TB6612 boards sit in the centre bay.
  - The UBEC sits in the left side bay and the ULN2003 in the right one, under the track.
  - The nose ESP32-CAM stands in grooves behind the nose, its lens in a window that leaves room for the lens position to vary between modules.
  - The Uno and ULN2003 screw onto standoffs; the battery, UBEC, and TB6612s are held to the floor with double-sided tape.
- **Top plate.** Snaps onto the chassis walls. It carries the raised track along the right-hand side, with a dovetail slot and a gear rack in its floor. It frames the work table, which drops into an opening and rests flush on a lip. A low rim on the table keeps objects the arm places there from sliding off while the rover moves. Lift the table out by its finger notch to reach the electronics; unsnap the plate for everything else.
- **Carriage.** Rides the track on dovetail runners. The 28BYJ-48 stepper on its motor plate drives a pinion on the rack, so the carriage, and the whole tower, travels 130 mm along the body.
- **Tower.** Screws onto the carriage deck and has three parts:
  - **Column.** Hollow. The PCA9685 servo driver screws to the inside of its inboard wall, reached through a snap-in door in its outboard wall.
  - **Hood.** Screws onto the top of the column and carries the yaw servo, shaft down.
  - **Lid.** Snaps onto the hood, for routing wires.
- **Arm.**
  - The yaw bracket hangs from the yaw servo's horn and holds the shoulder servo (DS3218).
  - The upper arm hangs from the shoulder and carries the elbow servo (MG90S).
  - The forearm carries the gripper servo (MG90S).
  - The gripper's gear drives two rack jaws in opposite directions, so the fingers stay parallel.
  - The arm camera sits in a cradle on the gripper, looking between the fingers.
- **Cables.**
  - The arm's leads run in printed clips along the links, into the hood, and down the column to the servo driver.
  - A flat ribbon of jumper wire carries the I2C bus, servo power, and the stepper's wires. It runs from the carriage along a channel beside the track, in a rolling loop that follows the carriage, and into the body through a port at the channel's front end.

### Reach

The arm reaches anywhere on the table, and the floor just ahead of the nose with the carriage at the front of the track. The named poses in `cad/src/harwood0_cad/arm.py` are the reach requirements; `make test-cad` checks that each one reaches its target, collides with nothing, and keeps the robot from tipping.

Tipping, from `cd cad && uv run harwood0-stability`:
- The robot weighs about 1.7 kg.
- Its centre of mass stays at least 59 mm inside the wheel contact rectangle in every named pose.
- The test requires at least 30 mm.

### Mecanum wheel orientation

The rollers touching the ground must form an X through the robot's centre: seen from below, each ground roller's axis points toward the middle of the robot. From above, the top rollers form a diamond. Front-left and rear-right use one roller hand; front-right and rear-left use the other. Mounted the other way round, the robot drives and strafes but can barely turn on the spot.

### Servo sizing

Measured off the CAD, the arm's worst-case static loads are:

| Joint | Servo | Stall torque | Hold, empty | Hold, 50 g | Hold, 100 g |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shoulder | DS3218 | 20 kg·cm | 0.9 kg·cm | 2.0 kg·cm | 3.1 kg·cm |
| Elbow | MG90S | 2.2 kg·cm | 0.25 kg·cm | 0.85 kg·cm | 1.45 kg·cm |

The elbow sets the payload limit. A servo holding a load continuously should stay under about a third of its stall torque, so the arm is rated for **50 g**. Objects up to 100 g are fine for short lifts close to the tower. The yaw servo turns about a vertical axis and carries no gravity load; the MG996R is there for its size and its stiff output.

### Print settings

- **Material.** PLA or PETG. PETG's springier snap arms and door tongues survive more cycles.
- **Layers and walls.** 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, and 4 top and bottom layers.
- **Infill.**
  - 20% gyroid for the chassis, top plate, and table.
  - 40% for the carriage, tower, hood, yaw bracket, and arm links.
  - 100% for the pinion, gripper gear, and jaws: small gears and racks need solid teeth.
- **Fit.** Print the `tolerance_coupon` first and tune `cad/src/harwood0_cad/params.py` before printing the large parts.
  - The coupon has three rows of five holes, each stepping 0.1 mm up from the end marked with grooves. The middle hole of each row is the current size.
  - **1 groove: M2.5 pilot** (`M2_5_PILOT_D`, 2.3 mm). Drive an M2.5 screw into each hole and pick the one it grips firmly without needing force or splitting the plastic.
  - **2 grooves: M3 pilot** (`M3_PILOT_D`, 2.8 mm). Test it the same way with an M3 screw.
  - **3 grooves: M3 clearance** (3.2 mm + `FIT_CLEARANCE`, 0.2 mm). Pick the smallest hole an M3 screw passes through freely. `FIT_CLEARANCE` also sizes every slot and sliding fit, so it is the most important of the three.
  - Shift each value by 0.1 mm for every hole away from the middle, and re-export.

## Wiring

### Pin map

The pin map is defined in `firmware/include/config.h`.

| Function | Uno R4 pin |
|---|---|
| FL motor PWM / IN1 / IN2 (front TB6612, channel A) | D3 / D2 / D4 |
| FR motor PWM / IN1 / IN2 (front TB6612, channel B) | D5 / D7 / D8 |
| RL motor PWM / IN1 / IN2 (rear TB6612, channel A) | D6 / D12 / D13 |
| RR motor PWM / IN1 / IN2 (rear TB6612, channel B) | D9 / A0 / A1 |
| Track stepper IN1–IN4 (ULN2003) | A2, A3, D10, D11 |
| I2C to the PCA9685 (SDA / SCL) | SDA / SCL pins beside AREF (shared with A4 / A5) |
| Reserved for an onboard computer UART (Serial1) | D0 / D1 |

Each TB6612's STBY pin is jumpered to its own VCC pin with a short female–female jumper, so the boards are always enabled; the firmware stops a motor by setting its inputs low. This frees the pin the I2C bus needed. Every other I/O pin is in use, so a new sensor has to go on I2C: the PCA9685 bus, or the Qwiic connector (3.3 V).

| PCA9685 channel | Connected to |
|---|---|
| 0 | Yaw servo (MG996R) |
| 1 | Shoulder servo (DS3218) |
| 2 | Elbow servo (MG90S) |
| 3 | Gripper servo (MG90S) |
| 4 | Arm camera power: V+ to the camera's 5V pin, GND to GND. The signal pin is unused. |

The PCA9685's VCC (logic) and GND come from the Uno's 5V and GND pins, with SDA and SCL, over the ribbon. Its V+ screw terminal takes servo power from the UBEC over two 22 AWG wires.

### Power

```
3S LiPo ── fuse ── switch ──┬── TB6612 VM (both boards)          motors, ~11.1 V
                            ├── Uno VIN
                            └── UBEC in ── 5 V ──┬── PCA9685 V+ terminal (22 AWG) ── servos, arm camera
                                                 ├── ULN2003 +
                                                 └── nose ESP32-CAM 5V
Uno 5V ──┬── TB6612 VCC (both boards, logic only)
         └── PCA9685 VCC (logic only)
```

Tie every ground together: battery, UBEC, Uno, both TB6612s, ULN2003, PCA9685, and both cameras.

The UBEC's 5 A rating covers the servos together:
- The DS3218 and MG996R each draw up to about 2.5 A at stall, and the MG90Ss about 0.7 A.
- Normal running draws well under 2 A in total.

Never power a servo or the stepper from the Uno's 5V pin. The arm camera shares the servo supply: if it resets when the arm moves hard, add a 470 µF or larger capacitor across V+ and GND at the PCA9685, in addition to the board's own.

## Assembly order

The order matters in a few places, marked **Order**: some screws can't be reached once the next part is on.

1. **Tolerance coupon.** Print the `tolerance_coupon` and tune the pilot sizes and `FIT_CLEARANCE` (see [print settings](#print-settings)).
2. **Chassis.**
   - Lower each motor in from above so its boss enters the motor-wall hole and its can rests on the saddle. Fit its two face screws.
   - Push the wheel couplings onto the shafts, following the [orientation rule](#mecanum-wheel-orientation).
3. **Electronics in the body.**
   - Screw the Uno and ULN2003 onto their standoffs.
   - Tape the TB6612 boards down, and fit each board's STBY–VCC jumper.
   - Tape the UBEC and battery down in their places (see the renders for the layout). Set the UBEC to 5 V.
   - Slide the nose ESP32-CAM down into its grooves, camera end up. Its lens hangs on a flex cable taped to the board: if it doesn't sit centred in the nose window, peel it off and re-stick it there, front face about flush with the nose.
   - Wire everything in the body: motors, power, the Uno to the TB6612s and ULN2003, and the UBEC to the ULN2003 and nose camera.
4. **Top plate.** Snap it onto the chassis. Drop the table in.
5. **Carriage.**
   - Push the pinion onto the stepper's shaft.
   - Slide the stepper in from the carriage's open inboard side and screw it to the motor plate by its ears.
   - Lower the carriage into the entry pocket at the rear of the track and slide it forward. Drop the two lip strips into the pocket (one turned half a turn) and screw them down.
6. **Ribbon.** Make up the ribbon. Feed its body end down the port, and its tower end up through the clamp tab's slot and the carriage deck.
7. **Column.**
   - Screw the PCA9685 onto the bosses inside the column, headers facing the door.
   - Thread the ribbon up through the column's open base, then screw the column's flange onto the carriage deck.
   - Plug the ribbon into the PCA9685: SDA, SCL, VCC, and GND to the bus header, and the 22 AWG pair into the V+ terminal.
8. **Hood.** **Order**: the yaw servo's tab screws go in from below, so fit them before the hood goes on the column.
   - Push the yaw servo up into the hood, shaft down, and screw its tabs to the floor.
   - Feed its lead down through the pass in the floor.
   - Screw the hood onto the column through the hood floor, with the lid off.
9. **Centre the servos** before fitting any horn, so each joint's zero lands where the firmware expects:
   - Flash the firmware, plug every servo into its channel, and power up.
   - Send `{"id":1,"cmd":"arm","args":{"yaw":0,"shoulder":0,"elbow":-85,"grip":45}}`. This is the centre of every joint's range, and every servo moves to its 1500 µs centre.
   - Fit each horn below in the pose described.
10. **Yaw bracket.**
    - Seat the yaw servo's disc horn in the pocket on top of the bracket and screw it on.
    - **Order**: fit the horn's centre screw onto the servo now, with the bracket pointing across the table (yaw 0). The shoulder servo blocks it afterwards.
    - Slide the shoulder servo in through the bracket's window, shaft first, and screw its tabs to the plate.
11. **Upper arm.**
    - Slide the elbow servo through its window, shaft first, and screw its tabs on.
    - Seat the shoulder's disc horn in the pocket on the upper arm's back face and screw it on.
    - Fit the arm onto the shoulder spline with the upper arm horizontal (shoulder 0), and fit the centre screw.
12. **Forearm and gripper.** **Order**: the gripper servo goes into the forearm before the gripper base covers its tab screws.
    - Screw the gripper servo into the forearm.
    - Screw the gripper base on from the forearm's back face.
    - Slide the two jaws into their grooves from the side, open the same distance (about 19 mm between the fingers, which is grip 45).
    - Put the horn, trimmed to 16 mm tip to tip, in the gear's back pocket. Fit the gear onto the spline between the racks, and fit the horn screw.
    - Slide the arm camera into its cradle.
    - Fit the forearm onto the elbow spline bent 85° down from the upper arm (elbow −85), and fit the centre screw.
13. **Arm wiring.**
    - Press the elbow, gripper, and camera leads into the link clips.
    - Route them behind the shoulder and over the yaw bracket into the hood, then down the column to channels 2–4.
    - Check every lead stays clear as you swing each joint through its range by hand, with the power off.
    - Snap on the hood lid and the column door.
14. **Calibrate:** see below.

## Calibration

Talk to the firmware over USB serial (115200 baud) or `nc <robot-ip> 7777`, one JSON command per line (see [protocol.md](protocol.md)). Change constants in `firmware/include/config.h` and re-flash with `make flash`.

- **Drive.**
  - With the wheels off the ground, send `{"id":1,"cmd":"drive","args":{"vx":0.3,"ms":1000}}`. Every wheel should turn forward. Flip `kMotorReversed` for any that turn backward.
  - Then check `"vy":0.3`: the robot should strafe left, which follows from the [orientation rule](#mecanum-wheel-orientation).
- **Track.**
  - Send `{"id":1,"cmd":"home"}` and watch the carriage: it should run to the rear end of the track, buzz against the end wall for a moment, then back off 2 mm. If it runs forward instead, send `stop`, flip `kTrackDirection`, and re-flash.
  - Then send `{"id":1,"cmd":"track","args":{"x":0}}`: the carriage should stop centred on the track.
  - Keep pinging during long moves; the API does this for you.
- <a id="servo-calibration"></a>**Servos.** The horns' splines only fit every 14–18°, so each joint lands within a few degrees of its target. Correct that per joint in `kServoCentreUs`, then check the scale:
  - Command the joint to its centre (as in step 9). Adjust `kServoCentreUs` until the joint sits exactly at its centre angle.
  - Command a second angle, for example shoulder 45 or yaw −45. If the joint turns the wrong way, negate its `kServoUsPerDeg`. If it overshoots or falls short, scale `kServoUsPerDeg`.
  - Finish by sending the park pose `{"yaw":0,"shoulder":45,"elbow":-130,"grip":0}`. Always park the arm before switching off: at power-up the firmware assumes the arm is parked.

## CAD workflow

The CAD lives in `cad/`, as build123d Python:

- `hardware/`: models of the purchased parts, for fit checks and renders only. They are never exported.
- `parts/`: the printed parts, one module each, registered in `parts/__init__.py` with their print orientations.
- `params.py`: print settings and the body's layout. `track_geometry.py` and `arm_geometry.py` lay out the track and the tower; every placement derives from them.
- `arm.py`: the arm's kinematics. Joint frames, forward and inverse kinematics, and the named poses.
- `assembly.py`: places everything in a pose, grouped into the chassis, table, and tower modules.
- `cables.py`: every cable run as a path through the moving frames, for clearance checks and the cut list (`harwood0-cables`).
- `stability.py`: mass, centre of mass, and tipping margin (`harwood0-stability`).
- `render.py`: offscreen PNG renders, each with six views.
- `show.py`: sends the same targets to OCP CAD Viewer for interactive inspection.

```sh
make render                                             # assembly, layout, every part and hardware item
cd cad && uv run harwood0-render assembly --pose ground_front
cd cad && uv run harwood0-render assembly --cables --section x=2   # cut away through the tower
cd cad && uv run harwood0-render upper_arm mg90s        # individual items
cd cad && uv run harwood0-show                          # the robot, chassis, and arm tower, in OCP CAD Viewer
cd cad && uv run harwood0-show print                    # every printed part on the bed, in a grid
cd cad && uv run harwood0-show --pose table_far --cables
```

To use OCP CAD Viewer, install the recommended VS Code extension. Open the viewer from its icon in the activity bar, or with "OCP CAD Viewer: Open viewer" in the command palette. It listens on port 3939, and `harwood0-show` sends whatever you name to it. The viewer's object tree lists every item by name, so you can hide the top plate or the column to see inside.

`make test-cad` checks that:
- every printed part is a valid solid that fits the print bed;
- key features exist;
- nothing in the assembly overlaps a printed part, in every named pose;
- the robot does not tip;
- each pose reaches its target;
- every cable has room at its real thickness, across a sweep of arm and carriage positions;
- the firmware's joint and track limits match the CAD's.

Render and test after every change.
