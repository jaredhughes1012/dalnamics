# Hardware

harwood0 is an indoor mecanum rover with a pan/tilt camera on top. It is built for easy assembly rather than strength: every part is screwed or pressed in place, with no glue, heat-set inserts, or bearings.

![Assembly](../cad/renders/assembly.png)

Renders are generated by `make render` (see [CAD workflow](#cad-workflow)).

## Bill of materials

References in brackets are item numbers from the original parts list.

## Layout

- **Chassis tray.** Four gearmotors mount transversely at the corners, bolted through the side walls by their gearbox faces, with each wheel on its motor's shaft. The battery lies lengthwise between the front and rear motors in floor corner guides. The UBEC sits beside it.
- **Deck.** The tray's lid, screwed to four wall bosses. It carries:
  - the Uno at the rear, with USB-C and the barrel jack facing backwards;
  - the two TB6612 boards on either side of the Uno;
  - the ULN2003 at front left;
  - the pan stepper on two pillars toward the front.

  Slots let the motor and power wires up from the tray. Lifting the deck gives access to the battery.
- **Camera head.** The pan head presses onto the stepper's double-D shaft and carries the MG90S. The servo's output shaft is the tilt axis, and it crosses the pan axis. The tilt bracket bolts to the servo horn and holds the ESP32-CAM in grooves. The camera sits about 20 mm to the right of the pan axis and 7 mm ahead of the tilt axis, and the API's camera extrinsics must account for this. Pan is limited to roughly ±180° by cable slack, and tilt to about ±45° by the fit checks.

### Mecanum wheel orientation

The rollers touching the ground must form an X through the robot's centre: seen from below, each ground roller's axis points toward the middle of the robot. From above, the top rollers form a diamond. Front-left and rear-right use one roller hand; front-right and rear-left use the other. Mounted the other way round, the robot drives and strafes but can barely turn on the spot.

## Wiring

### Pin map

The pin map is defined in `firmware/include/config.h`.

| Function | Uno R4 pin |
|---|---|
| FL motor PWM / IN1 / IN2 (front TB6612, channel A) | D3 / D2 / D4 |
| FR motor PWM / IN1 / IN2 (front TB6612, channel B) | D5 / D7 / D8 |
| RL motor PWM / IN1 / IN2 (rear TB6612, channel A) | D6 / D12 / D13 |
| RR motor PWM / IN1 / IN2 (rear TB6612, channel B) | D9 / A0 / A1 |
| TB6612 STBY (both boards) | A2 |
| Pan stepper IN1–IN4 (ULN2003) | A3, A4, A5, D11 |
| Tilt servo signal | D10 |
| Reserved for an onboard computer UART (Serial1) | D0 / D1 |

This uses every other I/O pin, so any new sensor has to go on I2C (the Qwiic connector) or displace something.

### Power

```
3S LiPo ── fuse ── switch ──┬── TB6612 VM (both boards)        motors, ~11.1 V
                            ├── Uno VIN
                            └── UBEC in ── 5 V ──┬── ULN2003 +
                                                 ├── MG90S V+
                                                 └── ESP32-CAM 5V
Uno 5V ── TB6612 VCC (both boards, logic only)
```

Tie every ground together: battery, UBEC, Uno, both TB6612s, ULN2003, servo, and ESP32-CAM. The 5 V loads peak around 1.5 A combined, well within the UBEC. Never power the servo or stepper from the Uno's 5 V pin.

## Assembly order

1. Print the `tolerance_coupon` and tune `FIT_CLEARANCE` if screws are loose or tight.
2. Chassis:
   - Lower each motor in from above so its boss enters the side-wall hole and its can rests on the saddle.
   - Fit the two face screws.
   - Push the wheel couplings onto the shafts, following the [orientation rule](#mecanum-wheel-orientation).
3. Drop the UBEC and the battery into their floor guides. Route the motor and power leads up through the deck slots.
4. Deck:
   - Screw the Uno and ULN2003 onto their standoffs.
   - Press the TB6612 boards into their guides.
   - Lower the stepper between the pillars, with its wire cover facing the rear, and screw it through its ears.
5. Screw the deck onto the chassis.
6. Pan head:
   - Slide the servo through the holder, shaft end first from the front, and screw its tabs down.
   - Centre the servo electrically before fitting the horn, so the bracket's zero is level.
   - Press the pan head onto the stepper shaft.
7. Tilt bracket:
   - Seat the horn in the bracket's back pocket and fit the bracket onto the spline.
   - Drive the horn screw through the bracket's centre hole.
   - Slide the ESP32-CAM down into the grooves, camera up and facing forward.
8. Wire it up according to the pin map and power diagram, leaving slack in the camera and servo leads for pan travel.

## CAD workflow

The CAD lives in `cad/`, as build123d Python:

- `hardware/`: models of the purchased parts, for fit checks and renders only. They are never exported.
- `parts/`: the printed parts, one module each, registered in `parts/__init__.py`.
- `params.py`: print settings and the rover's layout; every placement derives from it.
- `assembly.py`: places everything, and can pose the camera with `pan` and `tilt`.
- `render.py`: offscreen PNG renders, each with four views (isometric, front, side, top).
- `show.py`: sends the same targets to OCP CAD Viewer for interactive inspection.

```sh
make render                                             # assembly + every part and hardware item
cd cad && uv run harwood0-render assembly               # just the assembly
cd cad && uv run harwood0-render pan_head mg90s         # individual items
cd cad && uv run harwood0-render --only pan_head,tilt_bracket,camera --pan 90 --tilt 30
cd cad && uv run harwood0-show                          # interactive view in OCP CAD Viewer
cd cad && uv run harwood0-show pan_head --pan 90        # same targets and options as render
```

To use OCP CAD Viewer, install the recommended VS Code extension, then open the viewer from its icon in the activity bar or with "OCP CAD Viewer: Open viewer" in the command palette. It listens on port 3939; `harwood0-show` sends whatever you name to it. The viewer's object tree lists every assembly item by name, so you can hide the deck or battery to see inside.

`make test-cad` checks that every printed part is a valid solid that fits the print bed. It also checks that no two items in the assembly overlap wherever a printed part is involved, at several camera poses. Render and test after every change.
